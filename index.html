        💡 Hints for Best Biomechanical Results

        Follow these filming and setup tips to give Gemini crystal-clear frames and real measured joint angles rather than guesswork.

        🎥 1. Film in 120fps / 240fps Slow-Mo

        Even though frames are sampled as stills, high-speed phone video removes motion blur on the wrist, seam, and bat face—making every frame dramatically sharper.

        📐 2. Use 2 Complementary Angles

        • Side-on: Best for front-knee brace, stride length, head-over-front-toe, and bat swing arc.
        • Behind / Umpire POV: Best for alignment, falling away, arm path, seam presentation, and LBW line.

        Tap + Add Angle / Clip to analyze both together.

        🎯 3. Fill the Frame & Use a Tripod

        Place the camera closer—or use 2× + 🎯 Aim Zoom—so the player fills 60–80% of the view. Keeping the tripod in the same spot makes Before vs. After comparisons much more accurate.

        ✂️ 4. Tight Trim + Pin Key Phases

        Trim the Action Window to 1.5–2.0s around the action (20–30 frames), then step with -1f / +1f and tap the 📌 Pin Key Phase buttons (BFC, FFC, Release or Trigger, Stride, Impact) so Gemini sees the exact critical moments.

        🦴 5. Turn On Skeleton & 📐 Angle Tool

        Enable 🦴 Skeleton to track real joint angles (knee brace, elbow angle, trunk lateral tilt) directly in your browser and send those measured numbers to Gemini. Use 📐 Angle (tap 3 points: e.g. Hip → Knee → Ankle) to measure any custom angle on a paused frame.

        🧠 6. Add Ball Outcome & Pick Pro

        In Focus Areas & Notes, mention what happened to the ball (e.g. "dragging down leg" or "beaten on inside edge"). Use a Gemini Pro model for deep biomechanical or Before/After reviews, and Flash for quick net checks.
